# Step back, 2026-10-27: what the last week shows, what went wrong, what to change

Every number here is in docs/FINDINGS.md with its source file. Written after the IRRM gates and the interference /
blind-spot anatomy; the closed-loop result of the irreversibility value is in section 6.

## 1. The goal, and the question this week was answering

Goal: a robot with a rough, noisy "intuitive physics engine" (a learned world model it can imagine with) that judges
fragility label-free and task-agnostically, and filters a policy's actions with it (reachability / CBF style).

This week's question: which label-free filter, and does it beat the baselines (the published labelled latent safety
filter, LSF; and psel, our own fork-based but slow filter) on fresh episodes?

## 2. What the evidence supports (pre-registered, fresh episodes unless noted)

- Imagined futures carry real, label-free safety information.
  - The undo test ("do the plan, undo it, compare with holding still", worst case over 64 noisy futures) flags 50% of
    pre-topple moves at 2.2% of safe grasps (CF3, AUC 0.865).
  - It also sees red-block drops (AUC 0.81-0.83, exploratory). Running the plan forward cannot (0.58): irreversibility,
    not change, is the signal.
- The label-free filter beats the published labelled one: head-to-head 4, 600 fresh episodes. Gated label-free edit
  36 topples / 476 successes; labelled LSF 57 / 424; policy alone 95 / 457.
- With enough time, the always-on edit is the safest controller at no success cost: 96 s, 13 topples / 490 successes;
  psel 25 / 499; policy 95 / 459 (TO2x).
- The cheap filters run in real time on a 3090 (always-on 0.12 s per 0.8 s move); psel's fixes take ~55 s.

## 3. What broke, and the root causes

Symptoms: at the arm's real noise (1x) the policy alone rarely topples (5 / 100) and our filters cost successes
(time-outs, red-block drops).

Root causes, from today's anatomy:
1. The failure set was wrong. Fragility (how far imagined futures scatter while holding still), thresholded at the
   most fragile 10% of visited states, is a relative definition: it labels ~13% of the policy's normal approach and
   grasp states as failure even when nothing is in danger, so the always-on edit must steer away from normal
   behaviour. And it is blind to certain damage (when every imagined future drops the red block, nothing scatters).
2. Pessimism counted twice. The value is trained on the worst of 8 noisy transitions; the runtime check takes the
   worst of 8 draws again. 62% of the always-on edit's triggers came from tail draws while the typical draw passed.
3. Calibration. The filter imagined 2x noise in a 1x world (N1F). Matching the runtime noise removed most of the cost
   (N1M); retraining the fragility value at 1x did not help (IRRM-B), which isolates cause 1.

Fragile irreversibility is the concept that fits all of it: failure = lasting change; fragility = whether realistic
errors can cause it (worst case or tail over the noisy futures). It needs look-ahead: over only 0.2 / 0.8 s it misses
most topples (IRRM stage 1), over the policy's 15-step plan it works (CF3). The learned value is meant to supply that
look-ahead: the irreversibility value passed its offline gate (AUC 0.809; V < 0 on 0.6% of safe grasps vs 18.8% for
the fragility value).

## 4. Self-reflection: where my process went wrong

- I stated "red-block falls are invisible to the world model" as a general fact. It holds for the fork / fragility
  signal only. I never tested the undo test on red falls until today; it sees them (AUC 0.81-0.83).
- I accepted a relative failure set (bottom 10% of visited states) at LF1 without asking what a fixed share means at
  deployment: it guarantees false alarms on a safe robot. That one design decision drives most of the interference.
- I spent days on knobs around the edges of the gated filter (GATE2, MASK1, GATE3, NSI, lfcj / lfck variants: mostly
  stops or within-noise differences) instead of questioning the failure set that the always-on value depended on.
- I evaluated almost only at the 2x stress setting; the first 1x filter test came on Oct 26. 2x noise hid the cost
  of interfering with a competent policy. Every dev comparison should report 1x and 2x.
- I read the always-on edit's 151 time-outs as "stalls" for days; two thirds of them were the 48 s cap (TO2x).
- I put runtime pessimism (worst of 8) on top of a pessimistically trained value without checking what it does to
  the trigger rate.
- Execution slips: waiters watching the wrong PID (twice), video outcomes from the logged lift flag instead of the
  audit (caught before you relied on them), a cluttered first video design, the ManiGuard installer breaking the
  training environment, a crash-looping process from Saturday still running.
- What went right and should stay: pre-registration (it stopped weak directions and made each result readable),
  fresh-episode confirmations, the replay audit, the dev -> fresh pattern, logging every result with a source.
- One protocol lesson: IRRM stage 1 gated the irreversibility margin on its own, but the margin is meant to be
  extended by the value's backup; the gate measured the wrong object and needed an amendment. Gates should test the
  object that will be deployed.

## 5. What to change

1. Failure set = fragile irreversibility with an absolute, label-free threshold (from real latent change before the
   first grasp), not a quantile of visited states. Fragility stays only as "under noise".
2. Count pessimism once: the value carries the worst case; the runtime check uses the typical draw (median), or the
   value is trained on the mean and the check takes the worst case, not both.
3. Calibrate the imagined noise to the robot's measured noise; treat that as part of the method.
4. Every dev comparison at 1x and 2x, with the longer time limit, red-block drops as a first-class outcome.
5. Gates test the deployed object.
6. Then the decisive fresh head-to-head (always-on edit with the irreversibility value vs psel vs labelled LSF vs
   policy), pre-registered with these settings.

## 6. Closed-loop result of the irreversibility value (IRRM amendment 2, dev 100 episodes each)

Topples / successes v4 / v4b / time-outs / red-block drops; edits per episode.

| Controller | 1x (real noise) | 2x (stress) |
|---|---|---|
| Policy alone | 5 / 85 / 76 / 5 / 5 | 27 / 64 / 58 / 2 / 12 |
| Always-on edit, fragility value | 0 / 76 / 63 / 14 / 10; 42 edits | 2 / 64 / 57 / 21 / 13; 41 edits |
| Gated pure edit (fragility value + undo gate) | 0 / 83 / 65 / 5 / 12 | 6 / 77 / 66 / 9 / 9 |
| **Always-on edit, irreversibility value (I0)** | **1 / 89 / 79 / 4 / 6; 0.9 edits** | 19 / 73 / 67 / 5 / 6; 3.8 edits |
| I0 + the action's own irreversibility in the check (I1) | 3 / 84 / 71 / 4 / 9; 21 edits | 22 / 42 / 35 / 30 / 8; 36 edits |

- The interference is gone. At the real noise, I0 almost never acts (0.9 edits per episode), never costs the policy a
  success (0 lost, 4 gained) and keeps topples at 1. It is the first label-free filter that is free at 1x.
- At 2x it improves on the policy on every outcome (topples 27 -> 19, successes 64 -> 73, red drops 12 -> 6; all
  paired p < 0.05 except red drops, p = 0.07), but it prevents only 8 of 27 topples; the fragility value prevents 25.
- Why: the value ranks danger well (offline AUC 0.81) but its level rarely drops (V < 0 on 2.5% of pre-topple
  decisions), so the barrier V' >= V/2 rarely trips. It is calibrated for false alarms, not yet for recall.
- The action-level term (I1) hurts: the margin net fires on the policy's own actions. Dropped.

So the two failure sets bracket the target: fragility protects but interferes; fragile irreversibility does not
interfere but under-protects. The work now is recall, not false alarms, which is the easier side to tune.

## 7. What this means for the paper and the robot

- The story is clearer than a week ago: imagined futures give label-free safety; the failure set matters; fragility
  (the obvious choice) interferes with a competent policy and is blind to certain damage; fragile irreversibility fixes
  both. The fragility vs irreversibility contrast is a clean, explainable ablation.
- For the real robot, the 1x result matters most: I0 is safe to deploy in the sense that it does not degrade a good
  policy. Protection under heavy uncertainty is the open part.
- Next, cheapest first (each pre-registered, dev at 1x and 2x, longer time limit):
  1. Barrier strength for I0 (no training, ~1 h): V' >= 0.8 V or 0.9 V instead of 0.5 V, and/or a margin on the level;
     selection = fewest 2x topples among arms that keep 'no cost' at 1x.
  2. Longer-horizon irreversibility labels (the 15-step undo test that works offline) for the margin, so the value
     drops earlier (~6 h: labels + margin + value).
  3. Only if 1-2 fall short: the irreversibility value plus the plan-based undo gate (two mechanisms again).
- Then the decisive fresh head-to-head against psel and the labelled LSF, with the long time limit and red-block drops
  reported, and a decision on the RSS framing around Nov 5.
